# Psychology primer (orientation map — NOT a citable source)

Use this to know where to look. Every claim that ends up in an artifact must be re-sourced from `source-list.md` or new searches and verified in this run.

## The core bet
An app feels like it has a soul when the user experiences being **noticed, remembered and accompanied** — relatedness — while staying **in control** — autonomy — and **getting a little better** — competence. Gamification that trades autonomy for engagement (loss aversion, variable reward) can lift short-term opens but works against the very states a CBT app exists to treat (self-criticism, avoidance, rumination).

## Mechanism map

| Mechanism | Lever | Where to look | Healthy dose for CBT | Harmful extreme |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anthropomorphism | Face, gaze, name, turn-taking | Epley et al. 2007; Reeves & Nass 1996 (CASA) | Simple face, gaze toward user's content, few words | Over-trust; disclosure to a "friend" that is a product; perceived clinical authority |
| Parasocial bond | Repeated, consistent persona | Horton & Wohl 1956; Hartmann's PSI work | Same voice every day, small rituals | Loneliness substitution |
| Attachment | Secure base, reliable responsiveness | Bowlby; Darcy et al. 2021 (Woebot bond) | Predictable, calm, non-contingent warmth | Dependence; anxious checking |
| Relatedness (SDT) | Feeling cared for | Ryan & Deci 2000/2017; Peters, Calvo & Ryan 2018 | Reflect user's words, gentle care on hard days | Guilt as connection ("I missed you") |
| Autonomy (SDT) | Choice, rationale | Deci & Ryan; Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006 | Opt-in reminders, skippable everything | Controlling rewards crowd out motivation (Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999) |
| Competence (SDT) | Visible growth | same | Growth narratives, skills learned | Scores that judge feelings |
| Reciprocity / effort | App visibly works for you | Cialdini; Buell & Norton 2011 | Show the app "thinking" about your entry briefly | Fake effort theater |
| IKEA effect | User invests | Norton, Mochon & Ariely 2012 | User's own coping cards, own words | Sunk-cost lock-in |
| Affect labeling | Naming feelings reduces intensity | Lieberman et al. 2007; Torre & Lieberman 2018 | Granular emotion words in check-in | Labeling turned into a score |
| Validation | Being understood | Linehan 1997 (levels) | Acknowledge before suggesting | Toxic positivity |
| Variable reward | Unpredictable payoff | Skinner; Schultz 1997 | Rare, unscheduled warmth (not loot) | Compulsive checking |
| Loss aversion | Fear of losing | Kahneman & Tversky 1979 | Don't use | Streak anxiety; shame on break |
| Rituals | Structured repeated acts reduce anxiety | Brooks et al. 2016; Hobson et al. 2018 | 60-s opening/closing ritual | Ritual as obligation |
| Habit | Context-cued repetition | Lally et al. 2010 (median 66 days; missed days don't derail) | Forgiving cadence | Perfectionism framing |
| Self-compassion | Kindness to self after lapse | Neff 2003; Breines & Chen 2012 | Lapse copy models self-kindness | — |
| Guilt vs shame | Behavior vs self judgment | Tangney & Dearing 2002 | Never attribute lapse to the person | Shame copy on absence |
| Warmth/competence | Two axes of social judgment | Fiske, Cuddy & Glick 2007; Aaker et al. 2010 | Warm first, competent always (clinical accuracy) | Warm but wrong |

## Dose rule
For any mechanism borrowed from games or consumer apps, state three things in the artifact: (1) the original dose (e.g. Duolingo streak notification copy and cadence), (2) the CBT dose (what we keep), (3) the guard (what prevents the harmful extreme). Missing any of the three = RQ3.4 FAIL.

## Low-arousal default
Tsai's ideal-affect research distinguishes high-arousal positive (excited) from low-arousal positive (calm, content). For a mental-health product, the default emotional register is **low-arousal positive**; high-arousal celebration is reserved and rare.
