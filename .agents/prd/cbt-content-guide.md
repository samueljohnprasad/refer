## 1. Content Philosophy

### Teaching Approach: "Learn It, Spot It, Do It, Reflect On It"

Every topic follows a 4-step pedagogical loop:

```
📖 LEARN IT — Teach the concept with visuals and analogies (2-4 min)
     ↓
❓ SPOT IT — Quiz: Can the user identify the concept in real scenarios? (2-3 min)
     ↓
🏋️ DO IT — Exercise: Apply the technique to their own life (3-7 min)
     ↓
✍️ REFLECT — Journal: Write about the experience and what shifted (3-5 min)
```

### Content Rules

1. **No jargon** — Always provide a friendly name alongside the clinical term
   - "Cognitive Distortion" → "Thinking Trap"
   - "Cognitive Restructuring" → "Thought Reframing"
   - "Behavioral Activation" → "Action Boost"
   - "Dialectical" → "Both/And Thinking"
   - "Psychoeducation" → "Learn"
   - "Exposure Therapy" → "Facing Fears Gradually"

2. **Use analogies** — Every concept gets a memorable analogy
   - CBT Triangle → "The Three Gears" (thoughts, feelings, actions — they're all connected)
   - Cognitive Distortions → "Thinking Traps" (your brain falls into them automatically)
   - Thought Record → "Thought Detective" (investigating the evidence)
   - Graded Exposure → "The Fear Ladder" (climbing one rung at a time)
   - Mindfulness → "The Observation Deck" (watching thoughts go by like clouds)

3. **Maximum 40 words per learn card** — If you can't explain it in 40 words, split it into two cards

4. **Every card has a visual** — Illustration, icon, diagram, or animation. Never text-only.

5. **Real-life examples** — Always use relatable daily scenarios, not clinical case studies
   - ✅ "You check your phone and your friend hasn't replied. You think: 'They must be mad at me.'"
   - ❌ "Patient A presents with catastrophic ideation regarding interpersonal rejection."

6. **Tone** — Friendly mentor who's been through it. Not a textbook. Not a therapist.
   - ✅ "We all do this sometimes — it's just your brain trying to protect you."
   - ❌ "Cognitive distortions are maladaptive thought patterns."

## 2. The CBT Model — What Users Must Learn

### 2.1 Module: "How Your Mind Works" (Foundation — Every Journey Starts Here)

This is the mandatory first section of any CBT-based journey. Users must understand the model before they can use any technique.

#### Learn Nodes

**Learn 1: "The Three Gears" (CBT Triangle)**

| Card | Content | Visual |
|------|---------|--------|

| 3 | "Example: You think 'I'll fail this presentation' (thought) → You feel anxious (feeling) → You avoid preparing (action)" | Three gears with labels filling in |
| 4 | "The good news? You can change any gear to change the others. CBT teaches you how." | Highlighted gear with "You can change this!" |

| Card | Content | Visual |

## 3. Cognitive Distortions — The Core Curriculum

### 15 Thinking Traps (Each Gets Its Own Learn + Quiz + Exercise Sequence)

Each distortion follows this pattern:
1. **Learn Node** (3-5 cards) — Define it, give analogy, show 2 examples, key takeaway
2. **Quiz Node** (2-3 questions) — "Which trap is this?" scenario identification
3. **Exercise Node** — Apply to user's own life
4. **Journal Node** — Reflect on when they've experienced it

---

#### 3.1 All-or-Nothing Thinking

**Friendly Name:** "Black & White Thinking"  
**Analogy:** "Seeing the world like a light switch — everything is either ON or OFF, with nothing in between."  
**Clinical:** Evaluating situations in only two extreme categories rather than on a continuum.

**Learn Cards:**
1. "All-or-nothing thinking is when you see things in only two categories: perfect or terrible. There's no middle ground."
2. "Example: 'If I don't get an A, I'm a total failure.' The B+ doesn't exist in this thinking."
3. "Another example: 'I ate a cookie, so my diet is ruined. Might as well eat the whole box.'"
4. "Reality is almost always on a spectrum. Most things are somewhere between perfect and terrible."
5. "Key Takeaway: Watch for words like 'always', 'never', 'completely', 'totally' — they're clues."

**Quiz Questions:**
- Q1: "I messed up one part of the project, so the whole thing is garbage." → All-or-Nothing ✓
- Q2: "I didn't go to the gym today, but I did take a walk, so that's still something." → NOT a distortion (healthy thinking) ✓
- Q3: "Either I'm the best at my job or I'm worthless." → All-or-Nothing ✓

**Exercise: "The Spectrum Slider"**
- Step 1: Write a situation where you judged yourself as a total success or failure
- Step 2: Rate it on a slider from 0-100 (not just 0 or 100)
- Step 3: Write what a "middle ground" perspective would be
- Step 4: Rate how much you believe the middle-ground version (0-100%)

**Journal Prompt:** "Think of a time this week when you saw something as all-or-nothing. What would the 'gray area' version look like?"

---

#### 3.2 Catastrophizing

**Friendly Name:** "Worst-Case Spiral"  
**Analogy:** "Your brain is a disaster movie director — it takes a small problem and turns it into an apocalypse."  
**Clinical:** Expecting the worst possible outcome with certainty, or magnifying the importance of negative events.

**Learn Cards:**
1. "Catastrophizing is when your brain jumps to the worst possible outcome — and treats it as certain."
2. "Example: Your boss says 'Can we talk?' → Your brain: 'I'm getting fired → I'll lose my house → My life is over.'"
3. "It's like your brain has a 'what if' machine that only produces horror movies, never comedies."
4. "The antidote: Ask 'What's the MOST LIKELY outcome?' not 'What's the WORST outcome?'"
5. "Key Takeaway: Notice when your brain spirals to the worst case. Ask: 'Is this likely, or is this my disaster movie director?'"

**Quiz Questions:**
- Q1: "I have a headache, so it's probably a brain tumor." → Catastrophizing ✓
- Q2: "My flight might be delayed, so I should check the status." → NOT a distortion ✓
- Q3: "If I say the wrong thing at dinner, everyone will hate me and I'll never be invited again." → Catastrophizing ✓

**Exercise: "The Probability Check"**
- Step 1: Write down your worst-case fear about a current situation
- Step 2: Rate the probability of it actually happening (0-100%)
- Step 3: Write the MOST LIKELY outcome
- Step 4: Rate that probability
- Step 5: Write the BEST-CASE outcome
- Step 6: Compare all three — which one deserves your energy?

**Journal Prompt:** "Write about a time you catastrophized and the actual outcome was much less dramatic. How did you feel after?"

---

#### 3.3 Mind Reading

**Friendly Name:** "The Assumption Trap"  
**Analogy:** "Believing you have a crystal ball that tells you what other people are thinking — spoiler: you don't."  
**Clinical:** Assuming you know what others are thinking without evidence.

**Learn Cards:**
1. "Mind reading is when you assume you know what someone else is thinking — usually something negative about you."
2. "Example: Your friend doesn't text back. You think: 'She's annoyed with me.' But she's actually just busy."
3. "We're surprisingly bad at reading minds. Studies show we correctly guess others' thoughts only about 20% of the time."
4. "The fix: Replace 'I know they think...' with 'I don't actually know. I could ask.'"
5. "Key Takeaway: If you didn't hear it from them, you're guessing. And your brain's guesses are usually negative."

**Quiz Questions:**
- Q1: "He didn't laugh at my joke. He must think I'm boring." → Mind Reading ✓
- Q2: "She told me she was upset about the meeting." → NOT a distortion (she stated it) ✓
- Q3: "Everyone at the party is judging my outfit." → Mind Reading ✓

**Exercise: "Evidence Court"**
- Step 1: Write an assumption you've made about what someone thinks of you
- Step 2: List all evidence FOR this assumption
- Step 3: List all evidence AGAINST this assumption
- Step 4: Write an alternative explanation for their behavior
- Step 5: Rate your belief in the original assumption now (0-100%)

**Journal Prompt:** "When was the last time you assumed someone was thinking something negative about you? What other explanations could there be?"

---

#### 3.4 Emotional Reasoning

**Friendly Name:** "Feelings = Facts Trap"  
**Analogy:** "Believing that because you feel stupid, you ARE stupid. Feelings are real, but they're not always accurate reporters."

**Learn Cards:**
1. "Emotional reasoning is when you treat your feelings as proof of reality. 'I feel anxious, so something bad must be about to happen.'"
2. "Example: 'I feel like a fraud at work, so I must actually be incompetent.' (Hello, imposter syndrome.)"
3. "Feelings are valid — they matter. But they're not always accurate reporters of reality."
4. "You can feel terrified on a roller coaster while being perfectly safe. The fear is real; the danger isn't."
5. "Key Takeaway: 'I feel it' ≠ 'It's true.' Feelings inform, but evidence decides."

**Exercise: "Feeling vs. Fact"**
- Step 1: Write a strong feeling you've had recently (e.g., "I feel worthless")
- Step 2: If this feeling were testable in court, what evidence would support it?
- Step 3: What evidence would contradict it?
- Step 4: Rewrite: "I FEEL [X], but the FACTS show [Y]"

---

#### 3.5 Overgeneralization

**Friendly Name:** "One = All Trap"  
**Analogy:** "One bad apple and suddenly the whole tree is rotten."

**Learn Cards:**
1. "Overgeneralization is when one bad experience becomes a rule about everything. One rejection = 'Nobody will ever love me.'"
2. "Example: You fail one test → 'I'm terrible at everything.' One event becomes a life sentence."
3. "Watch for: 'always', 'never', 'everyone', 'no one'. These words are red flags."
4. "Key Takeaway: One event is one event. It doesn't define a pattern unless you have real evidence of a pattern."

**Exercise: "The Exception Finder"**
- Step 1: Write an overgeneralization you've made (e.g., "I always mess things up")
- Step 2: Find 3 specific exceptions where you DIDN'T mess up
- Step 3: Rewrite the statement more accurately: "Sometimes I make mistakes, and sometimes I succeed."

---

#### 3.6 Mental Filtering

**Friendly Name:** "Negativity Magnet"  
**Analogy:** "Your brain has a filter that only lets negative information through — like wearing sunglasses that only show shadows."

**Learn Cards:**
1. "Mental filtering is when you focus on the one negative detail and ignore everything positive."
2. "Example: You get 20 positive comments on a presentation and 1 critical one. You obsess over the 1."
3. "It's like your brain has a magnet that only attracts bad news."
4. "Key Takeaway: When you notice yourself dwelling on one negative, ask: 'What am I filtering OUT?'"

**Exercise: "The Full Picture"**
- Step 1: Describe a recent situation that felt negative
- Step 2: List ALL the negative aspects (what your brain focused on)
- Step 3: Now list ALL the positive or neutral aspects you might have filtered out
- Step 4: Rate the situation again considering the full picture (1-10)

---

#### 3.7 Disqualifying the Positive

**Friendly Name:** "Yeah, But..." Trap  
**Analogy:** "Catching a compliment and immediately throwing it away. 'They're just being nice.'"

**Exercise: "Compliment Jar"**
- Step 1: Write 3 positive things someone said to you or about you recently
- Step 2: Write the "Yeah, but..." thought that came after each one
- Step 3: Now write a version where you simply accept it: "Thank you. I'll take that."
- Step 4: How does it feel to accept rather than dismiss?

---

#### 3.8 "Should" Statements

**Friendly Name:** "The Rules Police"  
**Analogy:** "An inner drill sergeant with a rulebook about how you SHOULD be, MUST act, and OUGHT to feel."

**Learn Cards:**
1. "'Should' statements are rigid rules you impose on yourself and others. 'I should be better.' 'They should know better.'"
2. "When directed at yourself, 'shoulds' create guilt. When directed at others, they create anger and resentment."
3. "Example: 'I should be able to handle this without getting stressed.' → Guilt for being human."
4. "Try replacing 'should' with 'I'd prefer' or 'It would be nice if' — feel the difference?"
5. "Key Takeaway: 'Should' creates a gap between reality and expectation. Close the gap by adjusting the expectation."

**Exercise: "Should → Prefer"**
- Step 1: List 5 "should" statements you tell yourself regularly
- Step 2: Rewrite each using "I'd prefer..." or "It would be helpful if..."
- Step 3: Rate the emotional intensity of each version (1-10)
- Step 4: Notice which version feels more compassionate

---

#### 3.9 Labeling

**Friendly Name:** "Name-Calling Yourself"  
**Analogy:** "Slapping a permanent sticker on yourself based on one moment. One mistake → 'I'm an idiot.'"

**Exercise: "Behavior vs. Identity"**
- Step 1: Write a label you've given yourself (e.g., "I'm lazy", "I'm a failure")
- Step 2: Rewrite it as a behavior: "I procrastinated today" (not "I AM lazy")
- Step 3: Write evidence that contradicts the label
- Step 4: Create a more accurate, compassionate description

---

#### 3.10 Personalization

**Friendly Name:** "It's All My Fault"  
**Analogy:** "Taking responsibility for things that aren't your responsibility — like blaming yourself for the weather."

**Exercise: "The Responsibility Pie"**
- Step 1: Describe a situation you feel responsible for
- Step 2: List ALL the factors that contributed (other people, circumstances, timing, luck)
- Step 3: Draw a pie chart — what percentage is actually your responsibility?
- Step 4: How does this shift your feelings about the situation?

## 4. CBT Exercises (Core)

### 4.1 Thought Record (The CBT Gold Standard)

**What It Is:** A structured worksheet that helps users identify, examine, and reframe unhelpful thoughts.

**How to Teach It (Learn Node — 6 cards):**
1. "The Thought Record is the single most important CBT tool. It's like being a detective of your own mind."
2. "It has 7 columns. Don't worry — we'll fill them in one at a time, step by step."
3. "Column 1: Situation — What happened? Just the facts."
4. "Column 2: Emotion — What did you feel? Rate it 0-100%."
5. "Column 3: Automatic Thought — What went through your mind? What's the 'hot thought'?"
6. "Columns 4-7: We'll learn these in the exercise. They're where the magic happens."

**Interactive Exercise (7 steps, one per screen):**

| Step | Screen | User Input |
|------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | "Describe a situation that upset you recently." | Free text (2-3 sentences) |
| 2 | "What emotion(s) did you feel?" | Emotion picker (tap multiple) + intensity slider (0-100) |
| 3 | "What thought popped into your mind?" | Free text — the automatic thought |
| 4 | "What thinking trap might this be?" | Multiple choice from learned distortions (with hints) |
| 5 | "What's the evidence FOR this thought?" | Free text — list evidence |
| 6 | "What's the evidence AGAINST this thought?" | Free text — list counter-evidence |
| 7 | "Now write a more balanced thought." | Free text — reframed thought + re-rate emotion (0-100) |

**Quiz After (4 questions):**
- Q1: "What is the purpose of a thought record?" → To examine and reframe unhelpful thoughts ✓
- Q2: "Which column captures the 'hot thought'?" → Automatic Thought ✓
- Q3: Scenario: Fill in the missing column (give 6 columns, ask for the 7th)
- Q4: "Sarah wrote 'I always fail.' Which thinking trap is this?" → Overgeneralization ✓

**Journal Prompt:** "How did it feel to challenge your thought? Did your emotion intensity change? What surprised you?"

---

### 4.2 Cognitive Restructuring

**What It Is:** The process of identifying, challenging, and replacing negative thought patterns with more realistic ones.

**How to Teach (5 cards):**
1. "Cognitive restructuring is the art of reframing. Not positive thinking — REALISTIC thinking."
2. "It's not about saying 'Everything is fine!' It's about saying 'What's actually true here?'"
3. "The process: Catch the thought → Check the evidence → Craft a balanced alternative"
4. "Example: 'I'm going to bomb this interview' → Evidence check → 'I've prepared well. I might be nervous but I know my stuff.'"
5. "Key: The new thought must feel believable. If it feels fake, it won't work."

**Exercise: "The 3C Method"**
- **Catch:** Write the negative thought exactly as it appeared
- **Check:** Use Socratic questions:
  - "What evidence supports this thought?"
  - "What evidence contradicts it?"
  - "What would I tell a friend who had this thought?"
  - "Will this matter in 5 years?"
  - "Am I confusing a thought with a fact?"
- **Craft:** Write a balanced alternative that you actually believe (rate belief 0-100%)

---

### 4.3 ABC Functional Analysis

**What It Is:** Understanding the connection between Activating events, Beliefs, and Consequences.

**How to Teach (5 cards):**
1. "ABC stands for: Activating event → Belief → Consequence. It's the chain reaction of your mind."
2. "Something happens (A), you interpret it through a belief (B), and that creates an emotional/behavioral consequence (C)."
3. "Example: A = Friend cancels plans. B = 'She doesn't care about me.' C = Feel hurt, withdraw from friendship."
4. "Same A, different B: B = 'She must be overwhelmed.' C = Feel compassion, send a kind message."
5. "Key Takeaway: It's not the event that hurts you — it's your interpretation. Change B to change C."

**Exercise (3 steps):**
- Step 1: Describe a recent activating event (A)
- Step 2: Write the belief that formed (B) — what did you tell yourself?
- Step 3: What was the consequence (C) — emotion + behavior?
- Step 4: Now write an alternative belief (B2) — more balanced
- Step 5: What would the new consequence (C2) be?

---

### 4.4 Socratic Questioning

**What It Is:** A method of asking yourself probing questions to challenge assumptions.

**How to Teach (4 cards):**
1. "Socratic questioning is asking yourself the questions a wise friend would ask."
2. "Instead of accepting 'I'm not good enough,' you ask: 'Good enough for what? By whose standard? What evidence do I have?'"
3. "The 5 Power Questions: 1) What's the evidence? 2) Is there an alternative explanation? 3) What's the worst/best/most likely outcome? 4) What would I tell a friend? 5) What's the effect of thinking this way?"
4. "Key Takeaway: Don't believe every thought. Question it like a curious scientist."

**Exercise: "Question Your Thought"**
- User writes a troubling thought
- App presents each of the 5 Socratic questions one by one
- User writes a response to each
- Final screen: "Now re-read your original thought. Rate how much you believe it now (0-100%)"

---

### 4.5 Behavioral Experiments

**What It Is:** Testing negative predictions against reality through planned experiments.

**How to Teach (4 cards):**
1. "A behavioral experiment is like a science project for your brain. You have a hypothesis — let's test it."
2. "Example: Prediction: 'If I speak up in the meeting, everyone will think I'm dumb.' Experiment: Speak up once. Observe what happens."
3. "Before: Rate your belief in the prediction (0-100%). After: What actually happened? Re-rate."
4. "Key Takeaway: Your brain makes predictions. Test them. Reality is usually kinder than your imagination."

**Exercise (5 steps):**
- Step 1: Write a prediction your brain is making about an upcoming situation
- Step 2: Rate belief in prediction (0-100%)
- Step 3: Design a small experiment to test it (app suggests options)
- Step 4: After the experiment — what actually happened?
- Step 5: Re-rate belief. What did you learn?

---

### 4.6 Worry Time / Worry Postponement

**What It Is:** Designating a specific 15-minute "worry window" to contain worry to one time slot.

**Exercise:**
- Step 1: Set a daily "worry time" (e.g., 6:00 PM for 15 minutes)
- Step 2: Throughout the day, when a worry appears, write it down and say "I'll think about this at 6 PM"
- Step 3: At worry time, review the list. Cross off anything that resolved itself.
- Step 4: For remaining worries, apply: Can I do something? → Action plan. Can't do anything? → Acceptance exercise.
- Step 5: Reflect: How many worries were still relevant by 6 PM?

## 5. DBT Skills Exercises

### 5.1 Distress Tolerance

#### TIPP (Temperature, Intense Exercise, Paced Breathing, Paired Muscle Relaxation)

**How to Teach (4 cards):**
1. "When emotions hit crisis level, your thinking brain goes offline. TIPP is your emergency reset button."
2. "T = Temperature: Cold water on face activates the 'dive reflex' and slows your heart in seconds."
3. "I = Intense exercise: 10 jumping jacks or a fast walk burns off the adrenaline."
4. "P+P = Paced breathing (breathe out longer than in) + Paired muscle relaxation (tense then release)."

**Exercise: Guided TIPP**
- Step 1: Rate current distress (0-10)
- Step 2: Choose a TIPP technique → app guides through it (timer, animation)
- Step 3: Re-rate distress (0-10)
- Step 4: Log which technique worked best

#### STOP Skill

**Exercise (guided):**
- **S**top — "Freeze. Don't react. Just pause." (3-second pause with animation)
- **T**ake a breath — Guided deep breath (4 counts in, 6 counts out) with animated circle
- **O**bserve — "What are you feeling? What are you thinking? What's happening around you?" (user writes observations)
- **P**roceed — "Now choose what to do wisely. What's one skillful action?" (user writes action)

#### Radical Acceptance

**Learn (5 cards):**
1. "Radical acceptance is choosing to stop fighting reality. Not liking it — accepting that it IS."
2. "Pain is inevitable. Suffering = Pain + Non-acceptance. You can reduce suffering by accepting reality."
3. "It's NOT approval, agreement, or giving up. It's saying 'This is what is. Now what?'"
4. "Example: 'I didn't get the promotion. I can rage about unfairness, or I can accept it and plan my next move.'"
5. "Key Takeaway: Fighting reality doesn't change it. Acceptance frees your energy for what you CAN control."

**Exercise: "Turning the Mind"**
- Step 1: Write something you're struggling to accept right now
- Step 2: Complete: "The reality is: ___"
- Step 3: Complete: "I am choosing to accept this because ___"
- Step 4: Complete: "Now that I accept this, I can: ___"
- Step 5: Rate your sense of peace (0-10)

---

### 5.2 Emotion Regulation

#### Opposite Action

**Learn (4 cards):**
1. "Every emotion comes with an urge. Fear → avoid. Anger → attack. Sadness → withdraw."
2. "Sometimes the urge is justified. But often it makes things worse."
3. "Opposite action: When the emotion's urge is unjustified, do the OPPOSITE of what it's telling you."
4. "Sad → urge to isolate → Opposite: reach out to someone. Scared → urge to avoid → Opposite: approach."

**Exercise:**
- Step 1: What emotion are you feeling right now? (emotion picker)
- Step 2: What's the urge? What does the emotion want you to do?
- Step 3: Is acting on this urge helpful or harmful right now?
- Step 4: If harmful → What's the opposite action?
- Step 5: Commit to one opposite action. Set a reminder.

#### Check the Facts

**Exercise:**
- Step 1: Name the emotion (emotion picker)
- Step 2: What event triggered it?
- Step 3: What are your interpretations/assumptions about the event?
- Step 4: Are there other possible interpretations? List 3.
- Step 5: What are the actual facts? (not feelings, not assumptions — facts)
- Step 6: Does the intensity of your emotion match the actual facts?

### 5.3 Interpersonal Effectiveness — DEAR MAN

**Learn (6 cards):**
1. "DEAR MAN is a script for asking for what you need while keeping the relationship intact."
2. "D = Describe the situation factually. E = Express how you feel about it."
3. "A = Assert what you need. R = Reinforce — explain why it benefits them too."
4. "M = Mindful — stay on topic, don't get sidetracked. A = Appear confident."
5. "N = Negotiate — be willing to give and take."
6. "Example: 'When you cancel plans last minute (D), I feel unimportant (E). I need more notice (A). It would help us plan better together (R).'"

**Exercise: "Script Your Ask"**
- User fills in each DEAR MAN letter for a real situation
- App formats it into a practice script
- User can rehearse by reading it aloud (voice recording option)

### 5.4 Mindfulness — Wise Mind

**Learn (4 cards):**
1. "You have three minds: Reasonable Mind (pure logic), Emotion Mind (pure feeling), and Wise Mind (the balance)."
2. "Reasonable Mind: 'The statistics say this is safe.' Emotion Mind: 'I FEEL like it's dangerous.' Wise Mind: 'I notice my fear, and I also know the facts.'"
3. "Wise Mind is the overlap — where logic and emotion meet. That's where good decisions live."
4. "Key Takeaway: When making a decision, ask: 'What does my Wise Mind say?'"

**Exercise: "Three Minds Check"**
- Step 1: Write a decision or situation you're facing
- Step 2: What does your Reasonable Mind say? (logical analysis)
- Step 3: What does your Emotion Mind say? (gut feeling)
- Step 4: What would your Wise Mind say? (honoring both)
- Step 5: Choose a Wise Mind action

## 6. ACT Exercises

### 6.1 Values Clarification

**Exercise: "Values Card Sort"**
- Present 30 value cards (Family, Adventure, Creativity, Security, Health, etc.)
- User drags into three piles: "Very Important" / "Somewhat Important" / "Not Important Now"
- Narrow "Very Important" to top 5

### 6.2 Defusion — "Leaves on a Stream"

**Guided Exercise (Audio + Visual):**
- Close your eyes. Imagine a gentle stream.
- Each thought that appears — place it on a leaf and watch it float downstream.
- Don't push thoughts away. Don't hold onto them. Just observe.
- (60-second guided meditation with visual stream animation)
- After: "How do you feel?" (mood check)

### 6.3 The Passengers on the Bus

**Learn + Exercise:**
1. "Imagine you're driving a bus. The passengers are your thoughts and feelings."
2. "Some passengers are loud and scary: 'Turn back! You'll fail!'"
3. "You can choose to keep driving toward your values even with noisy passengers."
4. Exercise: Write your "destination" (a value or goal)
5. Write 3 "noisy passengers" (fears/doubts)
6. "Can you keep driving even with them yelling? What would that look like?"

## 7. Mindfulness & Somatic Exercises

### 7.1 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding

**Guided Exercise (interactive with haptics):**
- Name **5** things you can SEE (user types or taps photos)
- Name **4** things you can TOUCH (haptic pulse at each entry)
- Name **3** things you can HEAR
- Name **2** things you can SMELL
- Name **1** thing you can TASTE
- Final: "How present do you feel now?" (1-10)

### 7.2 Box Breathing (4-4-4-4)

**Visual Exercise:**
- Animated square that guides: Inhale (4s) → Hold (4s) → Exhale (4s) → Hold (4s)
- 4 rounds = ~2 minutes
- Haptic pulse at each transition
- Before/after calm rating (1-10)

### 7.3 4-7-8 Breathing

- Inhale (4s) → Hold (7s) → Exhale (8s)
- Animated circle expanding/contracting
- 3 rounds

### 7.4 Body Scan (Progressive Muscle Relaxation)

**Guided audio exercise (5 min):**
- Systematic: feet → calves → thighs → abdomen → chest → hands → arms → shoulders → neck → face
- Each area: tense (5s) → release (10s) → notice the difference
- Body diagram highlights each area as you progress
- Before/after tension rating (1-10)

### 7.5 Mindful Breathing (1-Minute Reset)

- "For the next 60 seconds, focus only on your breathing."
- Timer with gentle animation
- Count breaths (app counts for you)
- Single "return to breath" button when mind wanders
- After: "How many times did your mind wander? That's normal and okay!"

## 10. Exposure & Anxiety-Specific Exercises

### 10.1 Fear Ladder (Graded Exposure)
- List fears/avoided situations
- Rank them from least scary (1) to most scary (10)
- Start at the bottom rung: plan a small exposure
- After each: Rate anxiety before (0-10), during (0-10), after (0-10)
- See pattern: anxiety peaks then naturally drops (habituation curve)

### 10.2 Worry Decision Tree
- "Is there something I can do about this?" → Yes: Action plan. → No: Accept + coping skill.
- Interactive flowchart the user navigates

### 10.3 Interoceptive Exposure
- For panic: intentionally induce safe physical sensations (spin in chair, breathe fast)
- Learn: "These sensations are uncomfortable but not dangerous"
- Rate anxiety before/during/after

### 10.4 Decatastrophizing Worksheet
- Write the feared catastrophe
- "What's the probability (0-100%)?"
- "If it happened, what would you do?"
- "What's the most likely outcome?"
- "How will you feel about this in 1 week / 1 month / 1 year?"

## 11. Anger & Emotion Regulation Exercises

### 11.1 Anger Thermometer
- Rate current anger (0-10) on a visual thermometer
- Identify: What triggered it? What thoughts came with it?
- At each temperature level, learn a matching coping skill:
  - 1-3: Deep breathing
  - 4-6: Take a walk, splash cold water
  - 7-8: STOP skill, remove yourself
  - 9-10: TIPP technique (cold water on face)

### 11.2 Anger Exit & Re-Entry
- When anger rises: "I need a break. I'll come back when I'm calmer."
- Plan your exit phrase
- Plan what you'll do during the break
- Plan your re-entry: "I'm ready to talk about this calmly now."

### 11.3 Hot Thought Reframing (for Anger)
- Catch the angry thought: "They did this ON PURPOSE to annoy me"
- Challenge: What evidence? Alternative explanations?
- Reframe: "They might not have realized the impact."

## 12. Self-Compassion Exercises

### 12.1 Self-Compassion Break (Kristin Neff)
Three components:
1. **Mindfulness:** "This is a moment of suffering." (Acknowledge the pain)
2. **Common Humanity:** "Suffering is part of being human. I'm not alone in this."
3. **Self-Kindness:** "May I be kind to myself." Place hand on heart. What would you say to a dear friend?

**Exercise:**
- Write what you're struggling with
- Complete each of the 3 statements
- "What would you say to a close friend going through this?" → Write it
- "Now say that to yourself."

### 12.2 The Compassionate Letter
- Write a letter to yourself from the perspective of an unconditionally loving friend
- Prompt: "What would they say about this situation? How would they comfort you?"
- Read it back to yourself

### 12.3 Self-Criticism → Self-Coaching
- Write a self-critical thought
- Rewrite it as a supportive coach would say it
- Example: "I'm such an idiot" → "You made a mistake. Let's figure out what to learn from this."

## 15. Quiz Design — How to Test Each Topic

### Quiz Format Rules

1. **4-6 questions per quiz node**
2. **Question types (mix in every quiz):**
   - **Identify the trap:** Give a scenario → user picks the thinking trap / technique
   - **True/False:** Simple fact check on what was taught
   - **Fill-in-the-blank:** Complete a CBT model (e.g., "A → B → ?" in ABC model)
   - **Best response:** "What's the most helpful response in this situation?"
   - **Match pairs:** Match distortion names to examples
3. **Instant feedback** on every question (correct = green checkmark + brief explanation; wrong = gentle correction + explanation)
4. **Progressive difficulty:** Questions 1-2 easy, 3-4 medium, 5-6 harder
5. **Never punish:** Wrong answers still earn base XP, just miss the bonus
6. **Interleaving:** Later quizzes include 1-2 questions from earlier topics (spaced review)

### Sample Quizzes Per Module

#### Quiz: "The Three Gears" (After CBT Model lesson)

| # | Type | Question | Correct Answer |
|---|------|----------|---------------|
| 1 | True/False | "In the CBT model, changing your thoughts can change your feelings." | True |
| 2 | Identify | "Which 'gear' is this? Your heart is racing and your palms are sweating." | Feelings (physical sensations) |
| 3 | Best Response | "You feel anxious before a presentation. According to CBT, what's the most effective first step?" | Notice and examine the thought causing the anxiety |
| 4 | Fill-in | "Thoughts → ___ → Actions" | Feelings |

#### Quiz: "Thinking Traps" (After Cognitive Distortions)

| # | Type | Question | Correct Answer |
|---|------|----------|---------------|
| 1 | Identify | "'I failed one question, so I'll definitely fail the whole exam.'" | Catastrophizing + All-or-nothing |
| 2 | Identify | "'My coworker didn't say good morning. She must hate me.'" | Mind Reading |
| 3 | Match | Match 4 distortions to 4 scenarios (drag-and-drop) | — |
| 4 | Best Response | "Your friend says: 'I ALWAYS mess everything up.' What thinking trap should you help them notice?" | Overgeneralization |
| 5 | True/False | "Cognitive distortions are signs of mental weakness." | False — everyone has them |
| 6 | Identify | "'I feel stupid, therefore I must BE stupid.'" | Emotional Reasoning |

#### Quiz: "Thought Record" (After the exercise)

| # | Type | Question | Correct Answer |
|---|------|----------|---------------|
| 1 | Fill-in | "In a thought record, after identifying the automatic thought, the next step is to look for ___." | Evidence for and against |
| 2 | Best Response | "You wrote 'Nobody likes me' as your automatic thought. What's the best balanced alternative?" | "Some people seem distant, but I have friends who care about me." |
| 3 | True/False | "A balanced thought must always be positive." | False — it must be realistic, not necessarily positive |
| 4 | Identify | "Which column captures 'I'm going to fail'?" | Automatic Thought |

| **Gratitude Path** | Gratitude depth (word count, specificity over time), mood correlation | "On days you wrote specific gratitudes, your mood was 1.8 points higher." |

### Universal Report Sections

Every AI report includes:
1. **Mood Arc** — line graph of all mood checks across the journey
2. **Key Themes** — NLP-extracted top 3 themes from journal entries
3. **Technique Scoreboard** — which exercises the user rated most helpful
4. **Growth Metric** — one quantified improvement (e.g., "emotion intensity decreased 35%")
5. **Pattern Alert** — one recurring pattern the AI noticed (e.g., "You tend to journal about work stress on Mondays")
6. **Personalized Recommendation** — next journey suggestion with reasoning
7. **Shareable Card** — beautiful summary card (no private data) for sharing

### AI Safety Rules for Reports

- **Never diagnose:** "You may be experiencing..." NOT "You have depression"
- **Always data-backed:** Only report patterns that appear in the actual data
- **Always hopeful:** Frame insights as growth opportunities, not problems
- **Crisis detection:** If journal text contains crisis keywords → skip AI commentary → show helpline information
- **Privacy-first:** Reports stored locally by default. User opts in to cloud sync.
