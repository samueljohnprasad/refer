# Happy Product Guardrails

## Positioning

- Happy teaches mental-health concepts and supports self-guided practice.
- The core loop is Learn → Practise → Journal → Notice patterns → Continue.
- The product promise is: **Understand your mind. Practice what helps.**
- Calm, playful progression should reduce friction without making distress feel trivial.

## Health and claim boundaries

- Use educational and supportive language.
- Do not diagnose, prescribe, promise a cure, guarantee relief, or position Happy as a therapy replacement.
- Do not provide crisis intervention, emergency care, medication guidance, or personalised treatment advice.
- Market only capabilities verified in the released build.
- Treat clinical efficacy, expert endorsement, privacy, encryption, and outcome claims as requiring separate evidence and approval.

## Privacy and sensitive content

- Treat journal entries, voice, photos, and health-adjacent patterns as sensitive.
- Do not encourage public disclosure of personal reflections.
- Explain privacy and processing behavior accurately where users make or review reflections.
- Keep high-risk medical or self-harm content outside automated product guidance and route it for appropriate human or professional support.

## Experience rules

- First use asks what the user wants to understand or practise, not whether they have a condition.
- The selected path is reversible and must not restrict access to other courses or tools.
- The first path should connect one goal to one lesson, one related practice, and one reflection.
- Every lesson should lead to one relevant practice, reflection, or clear next step.
- Every practice should make completion and continuation understandable.
- Insights should show their time window and avoid diagnostic interpretation.
- Gamification supports returning and practice; it is not evidence that the product works clinically.
