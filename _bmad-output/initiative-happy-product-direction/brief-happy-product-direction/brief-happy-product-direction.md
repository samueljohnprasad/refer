# Product Brief: Happy

## Executive Summary

Happy is an iOS app for people who want to understand their thoughts, learn practical mental-health skills, and build a steady reflection practice. It combines short, guided courses with CBT tools, journaling, and personal pattern insights in one calm, playful experience.

The central experience is **Learn → Practise → Journal → Notice patterns → Continue**. A user can learn how anxiety, stress, sleep, or overthinking work; try a relevant exercise; reflect by text, voice, or photo; and return to insights that make the practice feel personal. Happy is self-guided education and support. It does not diagnose, replace professional care, or promise a clinical outcome.

The product direction is: **Understand your mind. Practice what helps.** The opportunity is not to make mental health feel trivial. It is to make useful learning easier to start, easier to practise, and easier to return to.

## The Problem

People dealing with anxious thoughts, stress, or poor sleep often have three separate needs:

- They want understandable education about what may be happening in their mind or routine.
- They want a concrete exercise they can use in the moment.
- They want to notice patterns over time instead of repeatedly starting from zero.

Existing options often solve only one part. Worksheets can feel dry or clinical. Mood trackers can record a state without teaching a next step. Meditation or wellness libraries can be broad and passive. Journaling can help, but a blank page creates friction and does not automatically turn reflection into learning.

The cost is not only inconvenience. People may abandon useful practices because the experience feels like homework, too much information, or another habit they have failed to maintain.

## The Solution

Happy brings the learning and practice loop into one mobile product:

- **Guided courses:** bite-sized lessons about anxiety, sleep, stress, mindfulness, and related mental-health topics.
- **CBT and coping tools:** thought reframing, grounding, breathing, worry exercises, and other guided practices.
- **Flexible journaling:** text, voice, photo, and structured prompts for reflection.
- **Personal insights:** daily, weekly, monthly, and longer-term views that help users notice recurring emotions, triggers, habits, and coping patterns.
- **Gentle progression:** roadmaps, checkpoints, streaks, and rewards that encourage returning without making progress feel like a competition.

The product should always make the next step clear: learn one idea, try one practice, or answer one useful prompt.

### Navigation and discoverability

Insights are the product's **Notice** step and must be a first-class destination in the primary iOS app shell, not a child of a generic Tracking route. Keep the tab bar focused to five destinations: **Home, Journal, Insights, Journeys, and Exercises**. Treat Capture as a prominent add action from Home and Journal rather than a permanent destination. Home should also surface an insight-readiness card after activation; before enough evidence exists, it should explain how to build toward a useful personal pattern instead of hiding the feature.

## What Makes This Different

Happy’s distinct position is the combination of three jobs in one coherent journey:

1. **Mental-health learning** explains the idea in plain language.
2. **Guided practice** lets the user apply a relevant CBT or coping skill.
3. **Reflection and insights** help the user connect the practice to their own life over time.

Gamification is a delivery system for consistency, not the product’s main promise. The product should feel more approachable than a worksheet and more substantive than a generic mood tracker.

This is an execution and experience advantage, not a claimed technical moat. Competitors may be stronger in clinical authority, meditation libraries, pet-based motivation, or fast mood logging. Happy succeeds only if its course quality, practice flow, reflection friction, and trust experience work together.

## Who This Serves

### Primary user

An adult or young adult who regularly overthinks, feels stressed or anxious, or struggles with sleep and wants a practical way to understand what is happening. They are curious, self-directed, and willing to practise if the app gives them a clear starting point. They do not want to be diagnosed or talked down to.

Success for this user means:

- They understand one useful idea about their experience.
- They complete a small practice instead of only consuming content.
- They can return to a journal or insight and recognise a pattern.
- They develop a repeatable routine without feeling punished for missing a day.

### Secondary users

People seeking structured self-reflection, sleep education, stress-management learning, or a gentle introduction to CBT-informed tools. The product must not imply that these users have a disorder or require treatment.

## Success Criteria

Record current baselines before setting numerical targets. The first success model is:

### User success

- Onboarding completion
- First lesson start and completion
- Lesson-to-tool usage
- Tool-to-journal completion
- Insight entry reach after the first meaningful loop
- First insight-readiness view and insight-open usage
- Day-1 and Day-7 return
- Repeat course or tool usage
- Journal entries that produce usable personal insight
- Low confusion, complaint, and unsafe-claim signals

### Business success

- Qualified App Store discovery and downloads
- Product-page conversion
- First paywall view and trial start
- Trial-to-paid and annual-plan selection
- Paid retention without harming early activation

The product should not use downloads, streaks, or subscriptions as proof of clinical benefit.

## Scope

### In scope

- Short educational mental-health courses
- CBT-informed thought and coping tools
- Sleep-reset and related wellness learning
- Guided text, voice, and photo journaling where available and clearly explained
- Personal reflection and pattern insights
- A first-class Insights destination connected to the Notice step of the core loop
- Calm, playful progression that supports practice
- Clear self-help boundaries, privacy communication, and professional-care signposting when needed

### Explicitly out of scope

- Diagnosis or assessment presented as diagnosis
- Claims that Happy cures anxiety, guarantees relief, or replaces therapy
- Crisis intervention or emergency care
- An open-ended AI therapist persona
- A social feed that encourages users to expose sensitive personal information
- Unsupported clinical efficacy, expert endorsement, privacy, encryption, or outcome claims

## Product Principles

- Teach before promoting.
- Make the next action small and specific.
- Connect every lesson to a practice or reflection.
- Use gamification to reduce friction, never to trivialise distress.
- Prefer pattern awareness over judgment or scoring.
- Be honest about what the app can and cannot do.
- Treat private reflections and health-adjacent information as sensitive.

## Vision

If Happy succeeds, it becomes a trusted personal learning journey for mental well-being: a place where people can learn how common thought and behavior patterns work, practise skills in small steps, reflect in the format that suits them, and build a clearer picture of what helps them over time.

The long-term opportunity is not to become a replacement for care. It is to make mental-health literacy and self-guided practice more understandable, more usable, and more consistent in everyday life.

## Known Unknowns

- Current activation, retention, conversion, and subscription baselines are not verified in this brief.
- App Store keyword rankings, ratings, and competitor performance are not treated as live evidence here.
- Clinical efficacy claims require separate evidence and review; this brief makes none.
- Privacy and AI-processing claims must be checked against the released build and current policies before marketing use.
- Further user research is needed to determine which first problem—anxiety, overthinking, sleep, or general self-understanding—creates the strongest activation.
