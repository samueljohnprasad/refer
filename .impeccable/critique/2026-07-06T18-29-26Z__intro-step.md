### Anti-Patterns Verdict

**The primary offense here is the "Identical card grids / Cards as the lazy answer" anti-pattern.**

Every single bullet point in the list has been wrapped in a bulky white card with a border, rounded corners, and a shadow (`bg-brand-surface border border-brand-border/60 rounded-2xl shadow-sm`). When you stack 5 of these on top of each other, it creates a heavy, ladder-like UI that feels cluttered and template-driven. It's the exact same "shape soup" we just fixed on the main feed.

*(Note: Deterministic scan found 0 structural CSS errors in `IntroStep.tsx` itself, but the visual execution fails the aesthetic heuristic).*

### Overall Impression
The copy and the illustration are charming and empathetic, but the structural execution of the list feels rigid and heavy. It feels like a settings menu rather than a calming introduction to a mental health exercise.

### What's Working
- **The Mascot & Typography:** The panda illustration and the large, serif "Thought Catcher" headline look beautiful and premium.
- **The Empathy:** The subtitle ("Notice a stressful thought? Let's catch it...") is excellent product writing.

### Priority Issues
- **[P1] The Stacked Card List:**
  - **Why it matters:** Putting simple text items into heavy, bordered, shadowed boxes creates unnecessary visual noise and cognitive load. It fights against the "quieter" aesthetic we want.
  - **Fix:** Strip the backgrounds, borders, and shadows from the list items. Make them a clean vertical list with generous spacing, perhaps connected by a subtle vertical line to show flow, or just simple text with the sage dot.
  - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill` or `$impeccable layout`

- **[P2] The Duration Pill Background:**
  - **Why it matters:** Just like on the feed, the solid `bg-sage-pill` draws the eye away from the primary title.
  - **Fix:** Make the pill transparent so it reads purely as typographic metadata.
  - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable quieter`

### Persona Red Flags
- **Alex (Power User):** The heavy cards make scanning slightly slower.
- **Casey (Mobile User):** The stacked cards eat up vertical space, pushing the "Continue" button further down.
