# Duolingo Course Structure and Teaching Methodology

## Course Structure: The Learning Path
Duolingo has evolved from its older "skill tree" design into a linear **Learning Path** designed to guide learners seamlessly. The curriculum is structured hierarchically:

1. **Sections:** The broadest level of organization. Sections group content by proficiency levels (e.g., CEFR levels A1, A2, B1) and represent major milestones in a course.
2. **Units:** Within each Section are Units. A Unit focuses on a specific theme, set of vocabulary, or grammatical concept (e.g., "Order food at a restaurant" or "Discuss travel plans").
3. **Nodes (Lessons/Steps):** Within a Unit, the path consists of individual stepping stones known as **Nodes**. Each node represents a specific activity:
   - **Regular Lessons:** The core exercises that teach new concepts.
   - **Stories (Storyteller Nodes):** Narrative-based comprehension exercises.
   - **Review/Practice Nodes:** Strategically placed exercises for spaced repetition.
   - **Chests/Rewards:** Nodes that grant XP boosts or gems.
   - **Unit Rewind/Trophies:** The final node of a unit that acts as a mastery test.

## Teaching Methods & Educational Psychology
Duolingo employs the **"Duolingo Method"**, which blends learning science, gamification, and machine learning:

- **Implicit Learning & Pattern Recognition:** Instead of explicitly teaching grammar rules upfront, Duolingo exposes learners to sentences and relies on the brain's natural ability to recognize patterns.
- **Active Recall:** Learners must retrieve information from memory (e.g., translating a sentence from scratch) rather than passively reading, which strengthens neural pathways.
- **Spaced Repetition (Birdbrain AI):** Duolingo's AI model tracks which words a user struggles with and schedules reviews at optimal intervals to counteract the "forgetting curve."
- **Storyteller Teaching (Stories):** Uses short, humorous dialogues to teach reading and listening comprehension in context. This bridges the gap between isolated vocabulary and real-world conversation.
- **Bite-sized Gamification:** Leveraging streaks, XP, leagues, and hearts to build a daily habit. The use of variable rewards keeps learners engaged.

## Types of Exercises
Duolingo keeps cognitive engagement high by rapidly switching between different exercise formats (often referred to as "cards" or screens):

1. **Translation:**
   - *Target to Native:* Reading/listening to the target language and translating it to the user's native language.
   - *Native to Target:* The harder variant, requiring the user to construct the sentence in the language they are learning.
2. **Matching / Pairs:**
   - Tapping pairs of words that mean the same thing (e.g., matching English words to Spanish words).
3. **Fill-in-the-Blanks:**
   - Selecting the correct missing word from a sentence to test grammar (like verb conjugations) or vocabulary in context.
4. **Listening Comprehension:**
   - Transcribing an audio clip in the target language (often with a "turtle mode" to hear it slower).
5. **Speaking Practice:**
   - Reading a sentence aloud into the microphone, evaluated by speech recognition software.
6. **Word Bank (Tap to Construct):**
   - A scaffolded translation exercise where users tap word tiles in the correct order to form a sentence, rather than typing it out freely.
7. **Multiple Choice:**
   - Selecting the correct image for a word, or the correct translation from a list of options.
