# B — Engagement & adherence in digital mental health / digital CBT

Run date 2026-11-09. All claims from sources retrieved this run (WebSearch result snippets of abstracts/landing pages; full texts NOT read unless noted). Confidence reflects snippet-level reading. "INFERENCE" = my reasoning, not a source claim.

## Q1. Documented dropout / retention numbers

- Real-world (non-trial) mental health apps: median 15-day retention 3.9% (IQR 10.3%) and 30-day retention 3.3% (IQR 6.2%) across 93 apps (median 100,000 installs), from panel-based usage data. — Baumel, Muench, Edan, Kane, JMIR 2019;21(9):e14567, https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567/, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Same study: by day 30, peer-support (median 8.9%), mindfulness/meditation (4.7%) and tracker apps (6.1%) retained better than breathing-exercise apps (median 0.0%). — Baumel et al., JMIR 2019, as above [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Real-world digital self-help for depression/anxiety (7 publicly available interventions, 11 papers): completion or sustained use (all modules / last assessment / use ≥6 weeks) ranged 0.5%–28.6%; real-world usage may differ from trial usage. — Fleming et al., "Beyond the Trial", JMIR 2018;20(6):e199, https://jmir.org/2018/6/e199, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- MoodGYM open access: of ~82,000 users in 2006, 27% completed one module and 10% completed two or more (program has 5 modules); school-based adolescents completed far more exercises (mean 9.38) than open-access adolescents (mean 3.10); adherence higher in monitored settings. — Neil, Batterham, Christensen et al., JMIR 2009;11(1):e6, https://jmir.org/2009/1/e6, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [medium — snippet-level]
- RCTs of smartphone mental health interventions (70 RCTs): mean attrition 24.1% short-term, 35.5% longer-term follow-up; lower attrition with acceptance-based interventions, monetary compensation, and reminders to engage; higher with online enrolment; usage consistently declined over trials. — Linardon & Fuller-Tyszkiewicz, J Consult Clin Psychol 2020, https://dro.deakin.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/Attrition_and_adherence_in_smartphone-delivered_interventions_for_mental_health_problems_a_systematic_and_meta-analytic_review/20730262, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- RCTs of depression apps (18 studies, n=3,336): pooled dropout 26.2%, rising to 47.8% after adjustment for publication bias; dropout lower with human feedback (11.74% vs 33.96% without) and with in-app mood monitoring; no difference CBT vs non-CBT or real vs placebo apps. — Torous, Lipschitz, Ng, Firth, J Affect Disord 2020, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31969272/, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Eysenbach's "law of attrition": in any eHealth trial a substantial proportion drop out or stop using; distinguishes nonusage attrition vs dropout attrition. — Eysenbach, JMIR 2005;7(1):e11, https://www.jmir.org/2005/1/e11, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Sleepio in IAPT: users signing up (n=510) had IAPT recovery 64.7% vs 58% controls (no completion rate retrieved). — source surfaced via search snippet, original publication not identified this run [grey/unverified] [low]

## Q2. Why engagement decays faster in mental health

- Systematic review (208 articles): common barriers = severe mental health issues hampering engagement, technical issues, lack of personalization; facilitators = social connectedness, increased insight into one's health, feeling in control, anonymity/privacy. Framework: user characteristics, content experience, technology/implementation environment. — Borghouts et al., JMIR 2021;23(3):e24387, https://www.jmir.org/2021/3/e24387, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Depression is characterized by behavioral avoidance, concentration difficulty, anhedonia and negative cognitions, which can impair mHealth engagement; adherence higher among those with lower average severity and those perceiving improvement. — Brighten study longitudinal analysis, arXiv 2412.00942, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00942v1, accessed 2026-11 [preprint] [medium]
- Scoping review of user engagement with digital interventions for depression (relevant lead; content not read). — JMIR via PMC9617183, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9617183/, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [low — not read]
- Eysenbach hypothesized nonusage and dropout share one mechanism ("losing interest"); proposed run-in designs for stable "hardcore users". — Eysenbach, JMIR 2005 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- "Healthy" disengagement: stopping can reflect skill acquisition/self-efficacy ("got what they needed"); episodic use during stressful periods identified. — grey clinical summary (omnicuris) citing a qualitative study, https://www.omnicuris.com/medshots/daily_updates/mental-health-apps-daily-self-care-clinical-guide_10-Oct-26_03-26, accessed 2026-11 [grey] [low]
- INFERENCE: The "e-attainment" idea (users stop because they improved) has only grey-level support from this run; treat as plausible, not established.

## Q3. Intention–behavior gap and designs to close it

- JITAI framework: 6 components — distal outcome, proximal outcomes, decision points, intervention options, tailoring variables, decision rules; aim is right type/amount of support at the right time by adapting to internal/context state. — Nahum-Shani et al., Ann Behav Med 2018;52(6):446–462, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5364076, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Implementation intentions added to eHealth programs did not increase adherence (MANOVA), though those who formed them completed more steps — student thesis, not peer-reviewed. — Boerkamp, Univ. Twente MA thesis, https://essay.utwente.nl/essays/81431, accessed 2026-11 [grey] [low]
- Plan reminders + implementation intentions in apps (HCI study, Interacting with Computers 2019) — lead, findings not read. — Univ. Birmingham, https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/investigating-the-impact-of-adding-plan-reminders-on-implementati/, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [low — not read]

## Q4. Guided vs self-guided; supportive accountability; bots

- Guidance increases completion (g = 0.29, 95% CI 0.18–0.40) and proportion completing (log OR 0.50) — small effects; full completion ~12% higher with guidance (22 studies). — Musiat, Johnson, Atkinson, Wilksch, Wade, Psychol Med 2022;52(2):229–240, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/impact-of-guidance-on-intervention-adherence-in-computerised-interventions-for-mental-health-problems-a-metaanalysis/02990B48E8FEB565569640C54BF8E9F1, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Baumeister et al. 2014: guided web interventions yield lower symptom severity and higher adherence (modules completed, completers). — Baumeister, Reichler, Munzinger, Lin, Internet Interventions 2014;1(4):205–215, https://libmast.utm.my/Record/doaj.art-6ab8a9b3756e4d31a093e54bf8d8d26b, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [medium — snippet; the "12% higher" figure in the snippet may belong to Musiat]
- IPD network meta-analysis (39 studies, 9,751 participants): guided > unguided iCBT at post-treatment (PHQ-9 MD −0.8, 95% CI −1.4 to −0.2), no difference at 6 or 12 months; difference small in subthreshold depression, larger when baseline PHQ-9 > 9. — Karyotaki et al., JAMA Psychiatry 2021, https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/168702/, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Supportive Accountability: human support raises adherence via accountability to a coach seen as trustworthy, benevolent, expert; process-oriented expectations set with the patient, explicit reciprocity, moderated by motivation, mediated by medium. — Mohr, Cuijpers, Lehman, JMIR 2011;13(1):e30, https://www.jmir.org/2011/1/e30/, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Woebot RCT (n=70, age 18–28, 2 weeks): mean 12.14 (SD 2.23) interactions; significant PHQ-9 reduction vs NIMH ebook control. — Fitzpatrick, Darcy, Vierhile, JMIR Ment Health 2017;4(2):e19, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5478797, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high; small, short trial]
- Wysa alliance (n=1,205): WAI-SR mean 3.64 → 3.75 after 3 days (n.s.); bond 3.98 → 4.05; transcripts showed gratitude, self-disclosure, personification; scores comparable to prior face-to-face studies. — Beatty, Malik, Meheli, Sinha, Front Digit Health 2022, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9035685, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed; company-affiliated authors] [medium]
- Torous 2020 (above): human feedback associated with much lower dropout (11.74% vs 33.96%). [peer-reviewed] [high]

## Q5. Ethics: engagement ≠ outcome

- Promote "effective engagement" — engagement sufficient to achieve intended outcomes — rather than more engagement; understand the engagement→behavior-change link. — Yardley et al., Am J Prev Med 2016;51(5):833–842, https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1528600/, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Dose-response: in an online depression RCT (n=280 active), only activities completed per log-in associated with clinically significant change; usage metrics explained little outcome variance. — Donkin et al., JMIR 2013;15(10):e231, https://jmir.org/2013/10/e231/, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- APA App Evaluation Model: accessibility, privacy/security, clinical foundation, engagement, interoperability; operationalized into 105 objective questions and an 8-question screener (2020). — Lagan, Torous et al., npj Digit Med 2020 and APA, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7393366, https://psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/mental-health-apps, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed + grey] [high]
- NICE ESF (updated Aug 2022): Tier 1+ DHTs must show user acceptability and engagement of relevant user groups in design/development/testing. — NICE ECD7, https://www.nice.org.uk/corporate/ecd7/resources/evidence-standards-framework-for-digital-health-technologies-user-guide-pdf-11696158815685, accessed 2026-11 [grey/regulatory] [high]
- Dark patterns in mindfulness apps affect perceptions, engagement and trust; exit dark patterns raise emotional cost of disengagement (Duolingo case, children). — deceptive.design summaries of academic studies, https://deceptive.design/articles/mindfulness-and-the-unseen-understanding-the-impact-of-dark-patterns-in-mindfulness-applications ; https://deceptive.design/articles/the-evil-bird-and-the-right-to-disconnect-children-s-vulnerability-to-exit-dark-patterns-in-gamified-educational-apps, accessed 2026-11 [grey summaries of peer-reviewed/preprint work] [medium]
- SilverCloud publicly states design ethics avoiding dark patterns (vendor position). — https://www.silvercloudhealth.com/uk/blog/design-ethics-for-mental-health-why-we-avoid-dark-patterns, accessed 2026-11 [grey] [low]
- INFERENCE: no peer-reviewed trial found this run showing streaks harm MH-app users; critique is mostly grey/HCI.

## Q6. Notifications

- Micro-randomized trial (n=1,255, 89 days, workplace well-being app): tailored push → 3.9% more likely to engage in next 24 h; largest at 12:30 pm weekends (+11.8%). — Bidargaddi et al., JMIR mHealth uHealth 2018;6(11):e10123, https://mhealth.jmir.org/2018/11/e10123, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Technology-based prompts vs none: RR 1.27 (95% CI 1.01–1.60, I² = 71%) for engagement; prompts promoting new content and sent shortly after start more likely to engage; borderline, interpret with caution (14 studies, n=8,774). — Alkhaldi et al., JMIR 2016;18(1):e6, https://jmir.org/2016/1/e6/, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Reminders associated with lower attrition in smartphone MH RCTs. — Linardon & Fuller-Tyszkiewicz 2020 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Users find repetitive notifications (e.g., for already-done tasks) annoying; many reminders can cause disengagement; customization of frequency/timing desired. — mixed HCI sources (AIT; Colorado State thesis), https://publications.ait.ac.at/en/publications/exploring-user-preferences-customisation-and-attitudes-towards-no/ ; https://mountainscholar.org/items/8c8cb36a-9cd5-49d3-9a52-8745feaa154f/full, accessed 2026-11 [mixed peer-reviewed/grey] [low-medium]

## Q7. Mood tracking / self-monitoring

- RCT (age 14–24, primary care): mobile self-monitoring of mood/stress increased emotional self-awareness, which indirectly reduced depressive symptoms (direct effect n.s.). — Kauer et al., JMIR 2012;14(3):e67, https://jmir.org/2012/3/e67, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- In-app mood monitoring associated with lower dropout in depression app RCTs. — Torous et al. 2020 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Tracker apps had among the higher 30-day real-world retention (median 6.1%). — Baumel et al. 2019 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- "Increased insight into health" is a facilitator of engagement. — Borghouts et al. 2021 [peer-reviewed] [high]

## Q8. Crisis / safety design

- Only 35% of mental health apps provided crisis-specific resources in the app interface; 10.5% had crisis language in ToS/privacy policies; crisis language inconsistent; recommend vetted, internationally accessible resources. — Parrish et al., Crisis 2021, https://escholarship.org/uc/item/43c2t9zt, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [high]
- Suicide-prevention app review: most apps emphasized support from friends/family (n=27) and safety planning (n=14); some contained potentially harmful content (e.g., lethal means). — Larsen et al. (Australian content analysis), https://www.pubmed.gov/27073900/, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [medium — author not confirmed in snippet]
- BeyondNow (Stanley-Brown Safety Planning Intervention as an app), n=36 tertiary patients, 2-month pilot: most viewed/edited safety plans and found app easy; feasible as adjunct to treatment. — Melvin et al. 2019, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6618059, accessed 2026-11 [peer-reviewed] [medium]
